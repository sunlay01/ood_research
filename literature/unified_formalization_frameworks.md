# Unified formalization frameworks

| Framework / paper | Common mathematical object | Algorithms covered | Excluded or awkward methods | Exact vs surrogate | Optimization treatment | Generalization theorem? | Source-only? | Main advantage | Main loss of fidelity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Risk-vector / multi-group robust learning (Sagawa2020) | vector `(R_1,...,R_E)` and simplex weights | ERM, GroupDRO, risk variance, worst-group | deep gradient statistics, conditional mechanisms | exact for finite groups | empirical optimizer; theorem abstracts solver | yes, finite groups | yes for observed groups | direct risk semantics | no model for arbitrary unseen domains |
| Discrepancy/IPM (BenDavid2010; Mansour2009; Gretton2012) | supremum loss discrepancy or RKHS mean distance | MMD, CORAL-like moments, domain classifiers | IRMv1 optimizer statistics | exact for discrepancy; representation is learned surrogate | class-level uniform convergence | yes, chiefly DA | source-only only when discrepancy is source-source and target family assumed | modular shift term | target samples/joint error often required |
| Invariant causal prediction (Peters2016) | invariant conditional `P(Y|X_S)` | ICP, stable/causal predictors, anchor variants | arbitrary feature alignment and gradient penalties | exact under structural assumptions | combinatorial/statistical test; no deep dynamics | yes under interventions | yes | interpretable mechanism | strong causal/coverage assumptions |
| Robust/DRO (Esfahani2018; Sinha2018) | uncertainty set `U(P_S)` and `sup_Q in U R_Q` | Wasserstein, f-divergence, group and moment DRO | methods without a defensible uncertainty set | exact robust objective; algorithm may use dual surrogate | convex duality or approximate solver | yes for robust population risk | yes, conditional on U | turns shift claim into one object | radius/metric misspecification controls validity |
| PAC-Bayes transfer (Germain2016; Rivasplata2019) | posterior `Q`, KL, disagreement/IPM | Bayesian DG/DA, randomized predictors | exact deep IRMv1/Fishr dynamics | theorem is posterior-level surrogate | posterior chosen/optimized abstractly | mostly DA; DG needs meta-prior | conditional | modular complexity + shift terms | prior and target-disagreement terms can be non-operational |
| Function-class + norm complexity (Bartlett2017) | `F` with norm/margin/Lipschitz control | spectral normalization, margin ERM | mechanism/causal semantics | exact class bound, not algorithm objective | optimizer ignored | same-distribution; combine with DG shift model | no by itself | scalable deep-class bounds | says nothing about admissible target family |
| Bilevel/meta-learning (Li2018) | update map / meta-train-meta-test loss | MLDG and validation-style DG | RKHS/distributional methods unless embedded | usually Taylor or linear surrogate | one/few-step or stationary-point assumptions | limited | yes | captures cross-domain training | second-order optimizer dependence is hard to concentrate |

## What unifiers sacrifice

Successful statistical unifiers choose a low-dimensional object with concentration or duality: risks, discrepancies, conditionals, posteriors, or uncertainty sets. They do not promise a lossless encoding of every optimizer. Gradient penalties can be written syntactically inside `L + lambda Omega`, but a useful theorem must also bound the derivative class, identify the relevant target family, and account for nonconvex optimization. The reviewed frameworks therefore classify such methods as surrogates or leave them outside the theorem.
