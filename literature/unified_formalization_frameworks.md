## What unifiers sacrifice

The cross-method papers and the translation paper make different tradeoffs explicit. Shui et al. choose invariance constraints and pay with conditional-density/representation assumptions; Wang & Veitch choose a structural causal model and pay with graph/intervention assumptions; Wang et al. (2026) choose a direct-sum latent representation and pay with deterministic invariant labels, localized discrepancy, and a restricted hypothesis class. Lai & Wang choose a variational TV functional and pay with regularity/coarea assumptions while leaving optimizer dynamics outside. This is different from a retrospective taxonomy: each paper states a common object or translation and then proves a result only for the subset that object controls.

Successful statistical unifiers choose a low-dimensional object with concentration or duality: risks, discrepancies, conditionals, posteriors, uncertainty sets, or a variational functional. They do not promise a lossless encoding of every optimizer. Gradient penalties can be written syntactically inside `L + lambda Omega`, but a useful theorem must also bound the derivative class, identify the relevant target family, and account for nonconvex optimization.
