# OOD/DG regularization taxonomy

Scope: source-only domain generalization (DG) unless a row says DA, and statistical objects at the population level. The displayed objectives are the authors' objectives or their direct population analogues; a theorem's surrogate is called out explicitly.

| Method / paper | Year | Family and canonical objective | Object regularized | Gradients / 2nd order | Target data | Level | Theory status and key caveat |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ERM (Vapnik; multi-source ERM) | 1998 | `min_f mean_e R_e(f)` | none beyond class complexity | no / no | no | function | Baseline; DG guarantee needs an environment sampling or admissible-family assumption [Vapnik1998]. |
| GroupDRO (Sagawa et al.) | 2020 | `min_f max_e R_e(f)` (empirical group risks) | worst-environment risk | no / no | no | function | Finite-group robust optimization; reweighting/optimization and group coverage assumptions matter [Sagawa2020, Sec. 3-4]. |
| IRM (Arjovsky et al.) | 2019 | `min_{Phi,w} sum_e R_e(w o Phi)` s.t. `w in argmin_barw R_e(barw o Phi)` | shared optimal classifier / invariant conditional | indirect derivative in IRMv1 / no | no | representation + function | Original paper proposes a constrained population principle; finite-sample results in later work usually restrict models or study ideal IRM [Arjovsky2019]. |
| IRMv1 (Arjovsky et al.) | 2019 | `mean_e R_e(w o Phi) + lambda sum_e ||grad_w R_e(w o Phi)||^2`, often `w=1` | first-order optimality violation | yes / no | no | parameterized surrogate | Gradient is w.r.t. classifier scalar/vector `w`, not network parameters; penalty is a surrogate for IRM constraint. This distinction drives theory limitations [Arjovsky2019, Eq. (3)]. |
| V-REx (Krueger et al.) | 2021 | `mean_e R_e + lambda Var_e(R_e)` | risk dispersion across environments | no / no | no | function | Population toy-model analyses and an extrapolation motivation; not a general deep-network target-risk theorem [Krueger2021]. |
| Fishr (Rame et al.) | 2022 | match per-example gradient variances, `sum_{e<e'} ||Var(g_e)-Var(g_e')||^2` | optimizer-statistic (gradient covariance) | yes / effectively 2nd-order statistics | no | parameter/statistic | Algorithm and causal motivation; no broadly accepted finite-sample target-risk theorem for the exact deep objective [Rame2022]. |
| CORAL (Sun & Saenko) | 2016 | `loss + lambda ||Cov(Phi#P_e)-Cov(Phi#P_e')||_F^2` | second feature moments | no / yes (covariance) | usually unlabeled target in DA; source-only variants compare sources | representation | Original CORAL is DA; population moment bounds require bounded features/concentration and do not imply conditional alignment [Sun2016]. |
| MMD | 2012 onward | `loss + lambda MMD(Phi#P_e,Phi#P_e')` | RKHS mean embedding | no / no | DA versions use target; DG versions source environments only | representation/function | IPM gives an estimable discrepancy and concentration; transfer bounds retain a joint-error term [Gretton2012; BenDavid2010]. |
| Adversarial domain discrepancy (DANN) | 2016 | `min_{Phi,f} max_d L_domain(d,Phi)` jointly with label loss | classifier-induced domain discrepancy | no / no | usually unlabeled target in DA; source-only needs source-domain adversary/family | representation | Domain-classifier dual is not the same mathematical object as MMD; target-data and discriminator-class assumptions enter [Ganin2016; BenDavid2010]. |
| Causal/invariant prediction (Peters et al.; ICP) | 2016 | select `S` with `P(Y|X_S)` invariant; test conditional hypotheses | conditional mechanism | no / no | no | statistical/causal | Guarantees are under structural interventions, faithfulness/coverage, and finite candidate-set assumptions; invariance alone is not identifying without them [Peters2016]. |
| Anchor regression (Rothenhäusler et al.) | 2021 | penalized covariance with anchor: `E[(I-P_A)(Y-Xb)]^2 + gamma E[P_A(Y-Xb)]^2` | shift sensitivity / anchor-explained residual | no / no | no (anchor observed in source) | linear function | Gives robustness over a specified shift family via a primal-dual result; not a generic DG theorem [Rothenhäusler2021]. |
| Wasserstein DRO (Sinha et al.; Esfahani & Kuhn) | 2018-2019 | `min_f sup_{Q: W(Q,P_hat)<=rho} E_Q ell_f` | worst-case distribution in uncertainty set | no / no | no | function/distribution | Duality converts robust risk to regularized loss under assumptions; guarantee is for the chosen ball, whose radius/metric encode the shift claim [Sinha2018; Esfahani2018]. |
| f-divergence / group DRO | 2016-2021 | `min_f sup_q {sum_e q_e R_e - rho D(q||p)}` | reweighting uncertainty | no / no | no | function | Clean convex duality and uniform convergence for finite groups; broad unseen-domain claims require an admissible family [Duchi2021; Sagawa2020]. |
| Spectral normalization / norm control | 2018 onward | constrain/product-penalize layer spectral norms, often margin loss bound | hypothesis capacity and Lipschitzness | no / no | no | parameter/class | Generalization and robustness bounds are class-level; they do not identify which target distributions are covered [Miyato2018; Bartlett2017]. |
| PAC-Bayes DG/DA | 2017-2022 | posterior `Q`: source Gibbs risk + `sqrt((KL(Q||P)+log...)/n)` plus shift/disagreement | posterior complexity and domain disagreement | no / no | DA variants may use target; source-only variants need domain prior/model | function/posterior | Bounds can be algorithm-dependent only through a chosen posterior; optimization is usually abstracted [Germain2020; Rivasplata2019]. |
| Bilevel/meta-DG (Li et al.; MLDG) | 2018 | train on meta-train environments, optimize meta-test loss: `min_theta L_meta-test(theta - alpha grad L_meta-train)` | cross-environment validation objective | yes / no | no | parameter/optimizer | Analyses typically use Taylor approximations, linear/convex models, or leave optimization error abstract; exact deep bilevel finite-sample DG bounds are rare [Li2018]. |

## Structural observations

1. “Regularization” spans three distinct objects: a risk vector `(R_e)`, a representation distribution `Phi#P_e`, and an uncertainty set over distributions. Gradient-statistic methods add a fourth, optimizer-statistic, level.
2. Moment/IPM, RKHS, DRO, and finite-group risk penalties are the most direct to concentration or convex duality. IRMv1/Fishr/meta-learning are objectives over derivatives of an inner risk or optimizer and are normally analyzed after replacing that object by a population constraint, a restricted model, or an abstract optimization error.
3. A source-only penalty is not itself a target guarantee. Every theorem needs either an environment-generating law, a shift/uncertainty set, a causal mechanism assumption, or an oracle joint-error term.
