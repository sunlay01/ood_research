# Shui2022 / On the benefits of representation regularization in invariance based domain generalization

## Problem
Marginal or conditional invariance alone may leave a prediction gap on an unseen domain.

## Target
Unseen-domain balanced error/risk.

## Representation
An `INV(Φ,S_1,...,S_T)` slot for marginal, feature-conditional, or label-conditional invariance, plus a representation contraction coefficient.

## Proof bottleneck
The target is not observed, so source invariance must be connected to a target family through a measurable shift distance.

## Proof-enabling property
TV triangle inequalities and strong data processing contract raw-space source-target TV by `alpha_TV(Φ)`, yielding a separable shift term.

## Main theorem
Proposition 1 bounds target BER by average source BER + conditional-invariance error `kappa` + `alpha_TV(Φ)·epsilon` for a nearest-source target family.

## Price of tractability
Bounded loss, conditional invariance, nearest-source family assumption and finite representation contraction; cross-entropy is generally unbounded.

## Algorithm relationship
POPULATION-ABSTRACTION: a common invariance framework, not exact empirical optimizer coverage.

## Scope
Naturally covered: marginal/feature-/label-conditional representation regularization. Excluded: Fishr gradients and arbitrary causal mechanisms.

## Source-only status
YES conditional on the target-family and invariance assumptions; `epsilon` is not observed in source-only training.

## Evidence
Eq. (1); Proposition 1 and proof; TV/Dobrushin definitions.
